# Omeprazole — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) that irreversibly blocks the gastric H+/K+-ATPase enzyme, reducing acid secretion. It is indicated for the short-term treatment (four to eight weeks) of active duodenal ulcer, gastric ulcer, and erosive esophagitis. It is indicated for the treatment and maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis and symptomatic gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). Omeprazole is also indicated for the reduction of risk of upper gastrointestinal bleeding in critically ill patients and, in combination with amoxicillin and clarithromycin (triple therapy), for the eradication of Helicobacter pylori to reduce the risk of duodenal ulcer recurrence. Pathological hypersecretory conditions such as Zollinger-Ellison syndrome are additional indications.

## Contraindications

Omeprazole is contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to proton pump inhibitors or any component of the formulation. Concomitant use with rilpivirine-containing antiretroviral products is contraindicated because PPIs raise gastric pH and reduce rilpivirine absorption dramatically, leading to treatment failure.

## Warnings and Precautions

Long-term PPI use is associated with several clinically important risks. Hypomagnesemia has been reported with prolonged use (typically longer than one year); it can present as tetany, seizures, or arrhythmia. Magnesium levels should be checked before initiating therapy in patients expected to be on prolonged PPI treatment, particularly those also taking digoxin or medications associated with hypomagnesemia. Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhea risk is increased with PPI use due to reduced gastric acidity and altered gut microbiome. Bone fractures (hip, wrist, spine) have been associated with high-dose or long-duration PPI therapy, particularly in older adults. Cutaneous and systemic lupus erythematosus-like reactions have been reported. PPIs reduce absorption of pH-dependent medications including ketoconazole, itraconazole, erlotinib, and atazanavir. Omeprazole is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C19 and can increase exposure to clopidogrel's active metabolite — this interaction is clinically debated; an alternative PPI (pantoprazole) is often preferred in patients on dual antiplatelet therapy. Vitamin B12 deficiency has been reported with long-term use.

## Dosing

For GERD and erosive esophagitis, typical adult doses are 20 mg once daily for four to eight weeks. Maintenance dosing is 20 mg once daily. For H. pylori eradication, 20 mg twice daily in combination regimens for ten to fourteen days. For Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, 60 mg daily, with doses titrated based on response.
