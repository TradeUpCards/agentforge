# Diabetes and Chronic Kidney Disease: Screening and Management

Adults with type 2 diabetes should be screened annually for chronic kidney disease (CKD) by measuring urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). CKD is defined as eGFR less than 60 mL/min/1.73 m² or UACR of 30 mg/g or higher persisting for more than 3 months.

SGLT-2 inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) are recommended for patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD (eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m²) because they reduce the risk of CKD progression and cardiovascular events independent of glycemic control. Finerenone, a nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, is recommended for adults with type 2 diabetes and CKD (UACR ≥30 mg/g and eGFR 25–75 mL/min/1.73 m²) to reduce CKD progression and cardiovascular events.

Blood pressure management is critical in diabetic CKD. ACE inhibitors or ARBs are first-line antihypertensive agents in patients with diabetes and albuminuria (UACR ≥30 mg/g) to slow CKD progression. These two drug classes should not be combined due to risk of hyperkalemia and acute kidney injury.

Metformin use should be reassessed when eGFR drops below 45 mL/min/1.73 m² and is contraindicated at eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m². Frequent monitoring of eGFR and electrolytes is essential in diabetic CKD patients on ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or SGLT-2 inhibitors.
