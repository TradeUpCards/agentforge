# Cardiovascular Risk Management in Diabetes: Lipids and Statin Therapy

Adults with type 2 diabetes aged 40–75 years should receive moderate-intensity statin therapy regardless of baseline LDL-C level because diabetes itself confers elevated cardiovascular risk. High-intensity statin therapy is indicated for patients with diabetes who have established cardiovascular disease or estimated 10-year ASCVD risk of 20% or higher.

LDL-C goals for most patients with diabetes and elevated ASCVD risk are less than 70 mg/dL. For patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), LDL-C should ideally be less than 55 mg/dL. If LDL-C targets are not reached with maximally tolerated statin therapy, addition of ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor is recommended.

Triglycerides greater than 500 mg/dL require immediate treatment to reduce pancreatitis risk, using fibrates (fenofibrate preferred over gemfibrozil due to fewer statin interactions), omega-3 fatty acids at prescription doses (icosapentaenoic acid), or dietary fat restriction.

Aspirin (75–162 mg/day) is recommended as secondary prevention in patients with diabetes and established cardiovascular disease. Aspirin for primary prevention in patients with diabetes is not routinely recommended due to uncertain benefit-to-risk ratio, particularly given bleeding risk.
