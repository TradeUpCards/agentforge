# Atorvastatin — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Atorvastatin is an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) indicated as adjunctive therapy to diet for reduction of elevated total cholesterol, LDL-C, apolipoprotein B, and triglycerides, and to increase HDL-C in adult patients with primary hyperlipidemia or mixed dyslipidemia. It is also indicated for the treatment of homozygous and heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. Beyond lipid management, atorvastatin is indicated to reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, revascularization) in adult patients with multiple risk factors for coronary heart disease, established coronary heart disease, or type 2 diabetes. It is used extensively in primary and secondary atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) prevention.

## Contraindications

Atorvastatin is contraindicated in patients with active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases. It is contraindicated during pregnancy — statin therapy should be discontinued as soon as pregnancy is recognized because cholesterol biosynthesis is necessary for normal fetal development. Atorvastatin is also contraindicated during breastfeeding. Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation is a contraindication.

## Warnings and Precautions

Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis are the most serious muscular adverse effects. Risk is increased by high doses, renal impairment, hypothyroidism, advanced age, and drug interactions — particularly with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, itraconazole, HIV protease inhibitors, grapefruit juice in large quantities). Patients should promptly report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness, and creatine kinase (CK) should be measured if symptoms develop. Liver enzyme elevations can occur; baseline liver function tests are recommended. There is a small but documented association between statin use and new-onset or worsening diabetes. Patients should be informed of this risk in the context of their overall cardiovascular benefit. Cases of cognitive impairment (memory loss, confusion) have been reported — generally reversible upon discontinuation.

## Dosing

Usual adult dosing range is 10–80 mg once daily, taken at any time of day with or without food. High-intensity statin therapy (40–80 mg) is recommended for patients with established ASCVD or high-risk primary prevention per ACC/AHA guidelines. Dose adjustments for renal impairment are generally not required. Dosing adjustments are needed when used with specific interacting drugs (e.g., dose cap of 20 mg daily with clarithromycin).
