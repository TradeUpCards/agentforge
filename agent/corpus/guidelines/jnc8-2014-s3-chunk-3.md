# Blood Pressure Treatment Thresholds and Targets (JNC 8)

The Eighth Joint National Committee (JNC 8) recommends initiating antihypertensive pharmacologic treatment in adults aged 60 years or older when systolic blood pressure (SBP) is 150 mmHg or higher, or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) is 90 mmHg or higher. Treatment goals are SBP less than 150 mmHg and DBP less than 90 mmHg in this age group.

For adults under 60 years, pharmacologic therapy is recommended when DBP is 90 mmHg or higher, with a goal of less than 90 mmHg. The same threshold applies to adults with diabetes or chronic kidney disease (CKD): initiate treatment when SBP is 140 mmHg or higher or DBP is 90 mmHg or higher, targeting SBP less than 140 mmHg and DBP less than 90 mmHg.

In the general non-Black population (including patients with diabetes), initial antihypertensive treatment should include a thiazide-type diuretic, calcium channel blocker (CCB), ACE inhibitor, or ARB. In the Black population without CKD, thiazide diuretics and CCBs are preferred initial agents.

For adults with CKD (regardless of race or diabetes status), initial or add-on treatment should include an ACE inhibitor or ARB to improve kidney outcomes. These agents should be used regardless of whether the patient is Black or non-Black when CKD is present.
