# Levothyroxine — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Levothyroxine sodium is a synthetic thyroid hormone (T4) indicated for the treatment of hypothyroidism of any etiology, including primary (thyroidal), secondary (pituitary), and tertiary (hypothalamic) hypothyroidism. It is also indicated as a pituitary TSH suppressant in the treatment or prevention of euthyroid goiters, including thyroid nodules, subacute or chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis (Hashimoto's disease), and multinodular goiter, and as an adjunct to surgery and radioiodine therapy in the management of thyrotropin-dependent well-differentiated thyroid cancer. Levothyroxine is not indicated for use in the treatment of obesity or for weight loss.

## Contraindications

Levothyroxine is contraindicated in patients with uncorrected adrenal insufficiency — thyroid hormone replacement increases the metabolic clearance of cortisol and can precipitate adrenal crisis. It is contraindicated in patients with untreated thyrotoxicosis of any etiology. Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation is also a contraindication.

## Warnings and Precautions

The most significant safety concern is over-replacement (iatrogenic hyperthyroidism), which can cause atrial fibrillation, reduced bone mineral density (especially in postmenopausal women), and cardiovascular strain. TSH should be monitored regularly — serum TSH is the primary monitoring parameter for primary hypothyroidism, while free T4 guides dosing in secondary or tertiary hypothyroidism where TSH is unreliable. Caution is required in patients with cardiovascular disease, including coronary artery disease and heart failure — therapy should be initiated at low doses with gradual titration to avoid precipitating angina or arrhythmia. Patients with diabetes mellitus may experience worsening glycemic control when levothyroxine is initiated or doses are changed. Numerous drug interactions affect levothyroxine absorption or metabolism: calcium carbonate, ferrous sulfate, proton pump inhibitors, antacids, bile acid sequestrants, and sucralfate all reduce absorption and should be separated by at least four hours. Levothyroxine should be taken on an empty stomach, ideally 30–60 minutes before breakfast, to maximize consistent absorption.

## Dosing

Dosing is highly individualized and weight-based. Full replacement in healthy adults is approximately 1.6 mcg/kg/day orally. In older patients or those with cardiac disease, therapy typically begins at 25–50 mcg/day with gradual titration every four to six weeks. TSH should be checked six to eight weeks after any dose change. Long-term stable patients typically require annual TSH monitoring.
