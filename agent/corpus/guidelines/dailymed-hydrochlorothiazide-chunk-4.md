# Hydrochlorothiazide — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) is a thiazide diuretic indicated for the management of hypertension, either as monotherapy or as part of combination therapy. It is also indicated for the treatment of edema associated with congestive heart failure, hepatic cirrhosis, and corticosteroid or estrogen therapy, as well as edema due to renal dysfunction including nephrotic syndrome and acute glomerulonephritis. HCTZ is one of the most widely used antihypertensive agents and is commonly paired with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or calcium channel blockers to achieve blood pressure targets.

## Contraindications

Hydrochlorothiazide is contraindicated in patients with anuria and in patients with known hypersensitivity to sulfonamide-derived drugs (HCTZ is a sulfonamide derivative). History of hypersensitivity to any component of the product is also a contraindication.

## Warnings and Precautions

Electrolyte imbalances are the primary safety concern with HCTZ. Hypokalemia is common and clinically significant, particularly in patients concurrently taking digitalis preparations, where hypokalemia can potentiate arrhythmias. Potassium supplementation or combination with a potassium-sparing diuretic may be needed. Hyponatremia, hypomagnesemia, and hypochloremic alkalosis can also occur. Periodic monitoring of serum electrolytes is essential. Hyperuricemia and precipitation of gout attacks can occur in susceptible patients. HCTZ may impair glucose tolerance and can worsen glycemic control in patients with diabetes; new-onset diabetes has been reported. Dose-dependent increases in serum calcium may occur — this is generally mild but warrants attention in patients with hypercalcemia or hyperparathyroidism. Photosensitivity reactions have been reported. Thiazides may reduce renal excretion of calcium, and long-term therapy has been associated with decreased bone resorption. HCTZ crosses the placental barrier and may cause fetal or neonatal electrolyte imbalances; use during pregnancy requires careful risk-benefit assessment.

## Dosing

For hypertension, the usual adult oral dose is 12.5–25 mg once daily. Doses above 50 mg daily are rarely used due to diminishing antihypertensive benefit and increasing electrolyte disturbance risk. For edema, doses of 25–100 mg daily may be used, often given intermittently. The lowest effective dose should be employed.
