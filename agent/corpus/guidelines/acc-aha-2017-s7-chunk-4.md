# 2017 ACC/AHA Hypertension Guideline: Revised Classification and Targets

The 2017 ACC/AHA guideline redefined hypertension as blood pressure of 130/80 mmHg or higher, lowering the prior threshold of 140/90 mmHg. This reclassification increased the proportion of US adults classified as hypertensive but emphasized that drug treatment is not necessarily required for Stage 1 hypertension (130–139/80–89 mmHg) in low-risk individuals.

For patients with Stage 1 hypertension and a 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk of less than 10%, lifestyle modifications alone are recommended as initial management. Drug therapy is reserved for Stage 2 hypertension (140/90 mmHg or higher) or Stage 1 hypertension in patients with known cardiovascular disease or 10-year ASCVD risk of 10% or higher.

Blood pressure targets for high-risk populations (cardiovascular disease, CKD, diabetes) are less than 130/80 mmHg under the 2017 guideline. Lifestyle interventions — weight loss, DASH diet, sodium reduction, aerobic exercise, and alcohol limitation — reduce SBP by approximately 4–11 mmHg each and remain first-line regardless of whether drug therapy is added.

ACE inhibitors, ARBs, thiazide diuretics, and calcium channel blockers are all acceptable first-line antihypertensive drug classes. Combination therapy with drugs from different classes is preferred over maximizing the dose of a single agent.
