# Glycemic Targets for Nonpregnant Adults with Type 2 Diabetes

The American Diabetes Association (ADA) recommends an A1C target of less than 7% for most nonpregnant adults with type 2 diabetes. This target balances the benefit of reducing microvascular complications (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) against the risk of hypoglycemia.

A less-stringent A1C goal, such as less than 8%, may be appropriate for patients with a history of severe hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy, advanced complications, or extensive comorbidities. Conversely, a more stringent target of less than 6.5% may be pursued if it can be achieved safely without significant hypoglycemia, particularly in patients who are younger, have shorter diabetes duration, or are treated with lifestyle alone or metformin.

Fasting plasma glucose targets are 80–130 mg/dL and 2-hour postprandial targets are less than 180 mg/dL for most adults. Time in range (TIR, 70–180 mg/dL) greater than 70% is an equivalent glycemic target metric supported by continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data.

A1C should be measured at least twice yearly in patients meeting glycemic targets and quarterly when glycemia is not controlled or therapy is changing.
