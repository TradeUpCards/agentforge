# Sertraline — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Sertraline hydrochloride is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) with FDA-approved indications for: major depressive disorder (MDD) in adults; obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 to 17 years; panic disorder in adults; post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in adults; premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) in adults; and social anxiety disorder in adults. It is among the most commonly prescribed psychotropic medications in ambulatory care settings.

## Contraindications

Sertraline is contraindicated in patients taking monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or within 14 days of stopping an MAOI — concurrent use risks life-threatening serotonin syndrome, with features including hyperthermia, rigidity, myoclonus, autonomic instability, and mental status changes. Concomitant use of sertraline with pimozide is contraindicated due to the risk of QT interval prolongation. The oral concentrate formulation contains a dropper that includes rubber; the formulation itself is contraindicated in patients with latex sensitivity. Concomitant use with MAOIs intended to treat psychiatric disorders is contraindicated.

## Warnings and Precautions

The FDA black box warning for sertraline (and all antidepressants) states an increased risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults up to age 24 during the initial weeks of treatment; close monitoring is required during this period. Serotonin syndrome can result from combinations with other serotonergic agents (e.g., triptans, tramadol, fentanyl, lithium, other SSRIs, SNRIs, buspirone, St. John's Wort). Activation of mania or hypomania may occur in patients with undiagnosed bipolar disorder. Abnormal bleeding risk is increased — SSRIs inhibit platelet serotonin uptake, and concomitant use with NSAIDs, aspirin, or anticoagulants further elevates this risk. Hyponatremia, possibly due to syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), has been reported particularly in elderly patients. QT prolongation has been reported at higher doses. Sertraline use late in pregnancy may be associated with neonatal adaptation syndrome (irritability, feeding difficulties, respiratory distress). Discontinuation syndrome can occur with abrupt cessation — symptoms include dizziness, nausea, sensory disturbances, and irritability; gradual tapering is recommended.

## Dosing

For MDD and OCD in adults, the initial dose is typically 50 mg once daily. The dose may be adjusted in increments of 25–50 mg at intervals of one week or more. The effective dose range is 50–200 mg per day. For PMDD, dosing may be continuous or limited to the luteal phase. No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate renal impairment; use with caution in hepatic impairment — lower doses are recommended.
