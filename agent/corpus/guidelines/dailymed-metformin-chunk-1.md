# Metformin — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Metformin hydrochloride is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus. It is the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for most patients with type 2 diabetes due to its established efficacy, weight-neutral or modest weight-reducing profile, low hypoglycemia risk when used as monotherapy, and cardiovascular safety data. Metformin is available as immediate-release and extended-release oral formulations.

## Contraindications

Metformin is contraindicated in patients with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) below 30 mL/min/1.73 m². It is also contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to metformin hydrochloride and in patients with acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis (with or without coma). Metformin should be withheld in patients undergoing radiologic procedures with iodinated contrast agents due to the risk of contrast-induced acute kidney injury and subsequent lactic acidosis.

## Warnings and Precautions

The most serious risk associated with metformin is lactic acidosis, a rare but potentially fatal complication. Risk is substantially elevated in patients with renal impairment, hepatic impairment, congestive heart failure requiring pharmacologic management, excessive alcohol use, and states of tissue hypoperfusion. Renal function (eGFR) must be assessed before initiating therapy and monitored periodically thereafter. The label recommends caution (not necessarily contraindication) when eGFR is 30–45 mL/min/1.73 m² — use only if benefit outweighs risk and monitor renal function more frequently. Metformin should be temporarily held perioperatively and in any acute illness associated with dehydration, hemodynamic instability, or sepsis. Vitamin B12 deficiency has been observed with long-term use; periodic monitoring is recommended.

## Dosing

Typical adult starting dose is 500 mg twice daily or 850 mg once daily with meals. Doses are titrated gradually (every one to two weeks) to reduce gastrointestinal side effects. Maximum recommended daily dose is 2,550 mg (immediate-release) or 2,000 mg (extended-release). Dose reduction or discontinuation is required when eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73 m².
