# Amlodipine — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Amlodipine besylate is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker indicated for the treatment of hypertension in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older. It is also indicated for the treatment of chronic stable angina and vasospastic (Prinzmetal's) angina in adults. Additionally, amlodipine is indicated to reduce the risk of hospitalization due to angina and to reduce the need for coronary revascularization procedures in patients with recently documented coronary artery disease by angiography. Amlodipine's long duration of action (elimination half-life approximately 30–50 hours) supports once-daily dosing with gradual, stable blood pressure reduction.

## Contraindications

Amlodipine is contraindicated in patients with known sensitivity to dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers or any component of the formulation. There are no other absolute contraindications listed in the FDA label, distinguishing it from verapamil and diltiazem, which are contraindicated in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) and conduction abnormalities. Amlodipine is generally considered safe in HFrEF based on outcome trial data, though it does not reduce mortality in this population.

## Warnings and Precautions

Symptomatic hypotension can occur, particularly in patients with severe aortic stenosis. Worsening angina and acute myocardial infarction have been reported at drug initiation or dose increase — this appears most likely in patients with severe obstructive coronary disease. Amlodipine causes peripheral edema (pitting edema of the lower extremities) as a dose-dependent adverse effect; this is a vascular effect rather than fluid retention and does not respond to diuretic therapy reliably. Use with caution in patients with severe hepatic impairment, as amlodipine is extensively metabolized by the liver and the half-life is prolonged significantly. Monitoring for excessive blood pressure lowering is recommended in elderly patients and those with hepatic dysfunction. Amlodipine has no clinically relevant drug interactions mediated through CYP3A4 inhibition at therapeutic doses, but CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole) can increase amlodipine exposure.

## Dosing

Usual adult starting dose for hypertension is 5 mg once daily; maximum dose is 10 mg once daily. For angina, 5–10 mg once daily. In elderly patients or those with hepatic impairment, initiation at 2.5 mg once daily is recommended. Effect is gradual — full antihypertensive effect is achieved over seven to fourteen days.
