# USPSTF Recommendation: Hypertension Screening in Adults (2021)

The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommends screening for hypertension in adults 18 years or older (Grade A recommendation). This is one of the USPSTF's strongest ratings, indicating high certainty that the net benefit is substantial.

Screening should be performed in a clinical setting using a properly calibrated sphygmomanometer with the appropriate cuff size. Elevated readings should be confirmed with office blood pressure measurement on one or more separate occasions before initiating treatment, or by ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) or home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) to rule out white-coat hypertension.

The optimal screening interval has not been definitively established. Adults with blood pressure that is normal (less than 120/80 mmHg) or elevated (120–129/less than 80 mmHg) should be rescreened annually. Adults whose blood pressure has never been abnormal and who have no cardiovascular risk factors may be screened less frequently (every 3–5 years), though the evidence is limited.

Adults with established hypertension who are receiving treatment should be monitored regularly (at least annually) to assess blood pressure control, medication adherence, and development of target-organ damage (kidney disease, left ventricular hypertrophy, retinopathy).
