# Pharmacologic Therapy for Type 2 Diabetes: Metformin and First-Line Agents

Metformin remains the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for type 2 diabetes management in most patients when tolerated and not contraindicated. It is effective, safe, inexpensive, and has a long track record of cardiovascular safety.

Metformin should be avoided or used with caution when the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) falls below 30 mL/min/1.73 m². When eGFR is 30–45 mL/min/1.73 m², metformin may be continued at a reduced dose with close monitoring of kidney function. At eGFR above 45 mL/min/1.73 m², no dose adjustment is needed.

For patients who do not achieve glycemic targets on metformin monotherapy, combination therapy should be considered. GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT-2 inhibitors are preferred add-on agents in patients with established or high risk of cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease, given evidence of cardiovascular and renal benefit.

Potential side effects of metformin include gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, diarrhea) that are dose-dependent and usually improve with gradual dose titration or use of extended-release formulations.
