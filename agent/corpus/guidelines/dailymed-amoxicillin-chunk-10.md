# Amoxicillin — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Amoxicillin is a semisynthetic aminopenicillin antibiotic with a broad spectrum of bactericidal activity. It is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of designated organisms in the following conditions: ear, nose, and throat infections (otitis media, pharyngitis, tonsillitis); lower respiratory tract infections (pneumonia, bronchitis); genitourinary tract infections; skin and skin structure infections; and as part of combination regimens for eradication of Helicobacter pylori to reduce the risk of duodenal ulcer recurrence. Amoxicillin is widely used in primary care and is the most commonly prescribed oral antibiotic in ambulatory settings in the United States.

## Contraindications

Amoxicillin is contraindicated in patients with a known serious hypersensitivity reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis or Stevens-Johnson syndrome) to amoxicillin, to other beta-lactam antibiotics (including other penicillins and cephalosporins), or to any component of the formulation. This is the single most important safety consideration: patients who report a history of penicillin allergy on intake forms must be evaluated carefully. True IgE-mediated penicillin allergy is the key concern; a history of anaphylaxis, urticaria, or angioedema to penicillin is a contraindication. A history of maculopapular rash (the most common penicillin "allergy" report) may be a low-risk reaction, and allergy evaluation or de-labeling by an allergist can be considered.

## Warnings and Precautions

Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported and can be fatal. Patients with a history of penicillin allergy have a higher likelihood of cross-reactive reactions to other beta-lactam antibiotics, including cephalosporins and carbapenems, though cross-reactivity rates are lower than historically believed. Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD) has been reported with virtually all antibiotics, including amoxicillin, and ranges in severity from mild diarrhea to fatal pseudomembranous colitis. Amoxicillin can cause a characteristic non-allergic maculopapular rash (particularly in patients with mononucleosis caused by Epstein-Barr virus or cytomegalovirus); this rash is not immunologically mediated and does not indicate true penicillin allergy. Prolonged amoxicillin therapy may result in overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms including fungi. Renal impairment reduces amoxicillin clearance; dose adjustment is recommended when creatinine clearance is below 30 mL/min. Amoxicillin may interfere with urinary glucose tests using copper reduction methods (Benedict's, Fehling's).

## Dosing

For mild to moderate infections in adults: 250–500 mg orally every eight hours, or 500–875 mg every twelve hours depending on the specific formulation and infection. For severe infections: 875 mg every twelve hours or 500 mg every eight hours. H. pylori eradication regimens typically use 1,000 mg twice daily in combination with a PPI and clarithromycin or metronidazole for ten to fourteen days. Dose reduction required in severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min).
