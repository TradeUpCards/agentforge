# USPSTF Recommendation: Screening for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes (2021)

The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommends screening for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes in adults aged 35 to 70 years who are overweight or obese (Grade B recommendation). This recommendation replaced the prior 2015 recommendation that began screening at age 40.

Abnormal blood glucose test results should be followed by referral to or provision of preventive interventions (intensive behavioral counseling programs) shown to prevent or delay onset of type 2 diabetes. Such interventions include structured lifestyle programs such as the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) model, which achieve 5–7% body weight reduction through diet and physical activity.

Screening tests include fasting plasma glucose (impaired if 100–125 mg/dL), 2-hour plasma glucose after a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (impaired if 140–199 mg/dL), or hemoglobin A1C (prediabetes if 5.7–6.4%). Any abnormal result should be confirmed with a repeat test before labeling a patient with prediabetes or diabetes.

Risk factors for type 2 diabetes that should prompt consideration of earlier or more frequent screening include family history of diabetes, history of gestational diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome, and membership in certain racial and ethnic groups with higher diabetes prevalence (African American, Hispanic/Latino, American Indian/Alaska Native, Asian American).
