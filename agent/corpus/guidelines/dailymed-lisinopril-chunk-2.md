# Lisinopril — FDA-Label Clinical Summary

## Indications and Usage

Lisinopril is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor indicated for the treatment of hypertension in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older. It is also indicated to improve survival in hemodynamically stable patients within 24 hours of acute myocardial infarction, and for the treatment of heart failure (as adjunctive therapy with diuretics, digitalis, and other agents) to improve symptoms and reduce hospitalization. Lisinopril is widely used as a first-line antihypertensive, particularly in patients with diabetes and/or chronic kidney disease due to its renoprotective effects on the glomerular filtration barrier.

## Contraindications

Lisinopril is contraindicated in patients with a history of angioedema related to prior ACE inhibitor therapy and in patients with hereditary or idiopathic angioedema. It is contraindicated in combination with a neprilysin inhibitor (e.g., sacubitril) — a 36-hour washout is required before transitioning. Concurrent use with aliskiren is contraindicated in patients with diabetes mellitus. Lisinopril is absolutely contraindicated during pregnancy due to well-documented fetal and neonatal toxicity.

## Warnings and Precautions

ACE inhibitors carry a black box warning for fetal toxicity: exposure during the second and third trimesters causes fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, limb contractures, delayed skull ossification, and neonatal renal failure. Pregnancy must be excluded before prescribing and women of childbearing potential must be counseled on risks. Angioedema of the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, or larynx can occur at any time — laryngeal angioedema is life-threatening. Black patients have a higher incidence of ACE-inhibitor-associated angioedema. Hypotension, particularly after the first dose, is a risk in volume-depleted or salt-restricted patients. Hyperkalemia can occur, especially in patients with renal impairment, diabetes, or those taking potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements. Renal function should be monitored after initiation and dose changes. Bilateral renal artery stenosis is a relative contraindication due to risk of acute renal failure.

## Dosing

For hypertension, the typical starting dose in adults is 10 mg once daily; range is 10–40 mg once daily. In heart failure, initial dose is typically 2.5–5 mg once daily, titrated to a target of 20–40 mg daily. Dose reduction is required in renal impairment based on eGFR.
